Security

See it first.
Sign it yourself.

Nerby Protocol reads the chain, previews transactions and prepares deployments. It never holds keys, and nothing is sent unless you sign it in your own wallet.

01 · No keys

The server cannot sign or send.

The safest way to protect a key is never to hold it. Nerby Protocol is built so that it cannot move funds even if it wanted to.

By design

No wallet on the server

The server has no private key, no seed phrase and no signing code. It never asks for yours.

By design

Tools that cannot send

The tools read the chain, compile code and estimate fees. None of them signs or submits a transaction to the network.

By design

You sign in your own wallet

When you deploy a contract, the deploy page shows the source, the fee and the address first. Your browser wallet signs and sends it; the transaction never passes through our server.

02 · Simulation

What a dry-run tells you.

Before you sign anything, your AI can run the transaction against the chain as it is right now.

You learn

Will it go through?

Success, or the exact revert reason the contract gives — for example an insufficient balance or a blocked address.

You learn

What will it cost?

A gas estimate and the fee at the current gas price, in the network’s native currency.

Keep in mind

A snapshot, not a promise

The chain keeps moving. Prices, balances and gas can change between the dry-run and your signature, so check the details in your wallet too.

03 · Your data

What is stored: nothing.

Stateless

No accounts, no sessions

There is no sign-up and no login. Each request is answered and forgotten; the application keeps no database.

Transparent

Where requests go

Your question is turned into a standard RPC call to a public node for the network you named. That node operator, and the hosting provider, can see the addresses being queried — as with any block explorer.

Public data

Only what is already on-chain

Everything returned is public blockchain data. Nothing private about you is needed to use it.

04 · Known limits

Read this before relying on it.

Nerby Protocol is new. These are the honest edges of what it does today.

  • It has not been audited. Treat results as a helpful first look, and confirm anything important on a block explorer.
  • AI can misread data. The numbers come from the chain, but the explanation comes from your AI model. Check amounts, addresses and decimals yourself before signing.
  • Deployments are final. A deployed contract cannot be edited or deleted, and the fee is not refunded. Check the network, the source and the numbers before you confirm in your wallet.
  • The token template is not separately audited. It is built on OpenZeppelin contracts, but the generated contract and anything you write yourself are your responsibility.
  • The endpoint is open and shared. There is no authentication, and the public nodes behind it apply rate limits, so heavy use can be slowed or refused.
  • A simulation cannot see the future. It does not protect against a contract that behaves differently later, or against approving a malicious contract.
  • Not financial advice. Nothing returned by the tools is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything.
